# Examples — read them after the first draft

These files are **demonstration material**, not source material for the user's file. They show the shape, proportions and voice of a finished output — and, for case 4, the source log and the echo built from it as a pair.

| Your case | Read |
|---|---|
| case 1 · full script / game text | `kei-soul.md` |
| case 2 · public material | `kei-soul.md` |
| case 3 · original character | `xiaoman-soul.md` (the earlier 小满, uncle's bookshop) |
| case 4 · chat logs → echo | `zhizimeng-echo.md` (output) + `hoshino-zhizimeng-chat.md` (source) |

## When to read

**After the first draft, before the first review round.** Not before drafting, and not during extraction.

The reason is contamination. Read too early, and the example becomes a source: its lines, names, proportions and turns of phrase leak into the user's file, and the result looks finished while resting on the wrong evidence. Read after the first draft, and it can only do what it is for — show what a finished section looks like, so the reviewer can point at the difference.

## The case-4 pair: source and echo

`hoshino-zhizimeng-chat.md` is the **source corpus**; `zhizimeng-echo.md` is the **echo built from it**. Read them as a pair:

- The chat log shows the **input shape**: one line per message, a timestamp, a speaker, only the two people's words, scene/time separators, and notes such as `（便条）` marked as not-instant. That is what "log-supported" looks like.
- The echo shows the **output shape**: what survived, what is marked, what it refuses to do, and how it answers rather than describes.
- Trace a few echo lines back to the log. That is the provenance check — the echo should be grounded in lines that are actually there, or clearly marked as supplied memory.

**Do not read the chat log before your own first draft.** It is the "before" half of the pair, and reading it early turns it into a source. Do not copy its lines into the user's file.

The chat log is a **fictional character** source (Blue Archive Vol.1 Chapter 3, Chinese translation), not a real person's private messages. The real-person boundary still applies: a real person's echo is for the person who built it and is not published.

## What not to do

- Do not copy lines, facts, names or phrasing from an example into the user's file. It is a shape reference, not evidence.
- Do not treat an example as a template to be filled. The ten H2s are the template; the content under them comes from the user's source.
- Do not copy from `hoshino-zhizimeng-chat.md` into a user's echo. It is the example's source, not the user's source.
- Do not use `zhizimeng-echo.md` to justify putting a **real person's** echo in a public place. The case-4 boundary still applies.

## The Xiaoman example is the earlier 小满

`xiaoman-soul.md` is based on the **earlier 小满** — fifteen, looking after her uncle's old bookshop — not the twenty-three-year-old underground idol from the later version. It has been rewritten to the current ten-H2 table: the old `别人眼里的我` material is now two scenes inside `我记得的事`, and `你眼中的我 / 我想变成的我` is one combined section. The two weight sections are intentionally empty: the older file predates them, and we did not invent entries. The blank carries the standard line. **Use it for voice and scene texture; do not copy its lines or facts.**

## One known difference in the Kei example

`kei-soul.md` writes the third H2 as **`先生眼里的我 / 我想变成的我`** instead of `你眼中的我 / 我想变成的我`. That is the same section, under a different addressee. **Ignore this one difference** when reading it as a shape reference; for a user who is not "先生", use the standard heading.

The examples are outputs from earlier versions of the method. Their headings may be Chinese-only, and the Kei and echo examples predate the current bilingual heading style. **Use them for shape and proportion, not for exact heading text.** The template in `SKILL.md` → Template is authoritative.
